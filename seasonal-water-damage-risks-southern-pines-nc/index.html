Seasonal Water Damage Risks to Check Yearly in Southern Pines

By the Southern Pines Water Damage Pros team

Water damage does not arrive on a calendar, but the causes do shift with the seasons. Checking the right things at the right time catches small problems before they become losses. This guide walks through the year for a Southern Pines home and points to the checks that matter most.

Winter: cold snaps and idle plumbing

Freezing nights do happen in the Sandhills, but a hard freeze is occasional, which is why pipes in garages, crawl spaces and exterior walls may have no protection. Before the first cold snap, insulate exposed pipes, disconnect and drain hoses and know where your main valve is. If you plan to travel in winter, set the heat to a safe minimum.

  • Open cabinet doors on exterior walls on freezing nights.
  • Let a faucet drip when a hard freeze is forecast.
  • Check crawl space vents and any gaps that let in cold air.
  • Look for frost on exposed lines in garages and utility rooms.

If a line does split, close the main valve and see our page on frozen pipe water damage.

Spring: storms and roof checks

Spring brings heavy rain and wind. Check gutters, downspouts and roof edges after winter debris, and walk the property for low spots near the foundation. Look at ceilings after the first big rain, because roof leaks show up later as stains. Check attic insulation for dampness.

Summer: air conditioning and irrigation

Summer demand for water is far above the yearly average. The Town of Southern Pines 2024 water quality report shows that the water plant averaged 3.5 million gallons a day, while its busiest day, June 27, reached 5.76 million gallons. That gap reflects outdoor watering and heavy use, and it is a reminder to check irrigation lines, hose connections and outdoor spigots for leaks.

Air conditioners also run for months. Check the condensate drain line and pan, and ask your HVAC technician about a float switch. See AC leak water damage for what happens when that drain fails.

Fall: tropical systems

Tropical moisture can reach Moore County late in the year. Chantal in 2025 showed how fast tropical moisture can arrive. Before the season peaks, clean gutters, test any sump pump and review your insurance. Flood coverage is a separate policy, according to the NC Department of Insurance.

Year-round checks that take ten minutes

  • Look under every sink for dampness or stains.
  • Check the floor around your water heater and washer.
  • Inspect supply hoses for bulges, cracks or rust.
  • Read your water meter, leave all fixtures off for an hour and read it again.
  • Check ceilings and the corners of rooms for new stains.

These small habits catch most slow leaks. A slow leak behind a wall or under a slab can run for months before it shows, so consider a water leak sensor near major appliances.

Pine needles, roofs and the Sandhills climate

The Sandhills are named for their sandy, fast-draining soil, which is a help during rain but not a shield. Water that cannot soak in quickly still runs along the surface toward low spots, patios and garage doors. Pine needles also collect on roofs and in valleys, where they hold moisture against shingles and block the path that water should take to the gutter.

A spring and fall roof walk

  • Look from the ground with binoculars for lifted or missing shingles.
  • Check valleys and the area around chimneys for built-up needles.
  • Look inside the attic with a flashlight after a hard rain for damp insulation.
  • Check that bathroom and kitchen vents terminate outside and are not leaking into the attic.
  • Look at the fascia boards for soft or stained wood, which can signal a long-term overflow.

None of this needs special tools. A fifteen minute walk twice a year catches many of the problems that later show up as ceiling stains.

Write the checks on a calendar. A reminder in March, June, September and December keeps the yearly cycle from slipping, and it takes a few minutes each time.

Source note for Southern Pines

The summer figures come from the Town of Southern Pines 2024 Annual Water Quality Report, which also states that the Town's drinking water source is surface water from Drowning Creek. For freeze alerts and rain forecasts, use the National Weather Service forecast for Moore County.

Homeowners in Aberdeen and Pinehurst can use the same yearly cycle.

Frequently asked questions

What is the biggest seasonal water risk in Southern Pines?

It depends on the home. Storms and roof leaks affect many in spring and fall, air conditioner condensate in summer and freezing pipes in the occasional hard freeze.

Should I drain outdoor hoses before winter?

Yes. Disconnect hoses and drain outdoor spigot lines before a freeze to prevent split fittings.

How do I check for a slow leak?

Read your water meter, turn off all fixtures for an hour, and read it again. If it moved, water is leaking somewhere.

Does summer use affect Southern Pines water demand?

Yes. The Town's 2024 water quality report shows the plant's maximum day, June 27, at 5.76 million gallons against an average of 3.5 million gallons.

Where does Southern Pines drinking water come from?

The Town's report says its supply is a surface water source at Drowning Creek.
