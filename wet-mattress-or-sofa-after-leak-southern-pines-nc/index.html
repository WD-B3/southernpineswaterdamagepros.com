Wet Mattress or Sofa After a Leak? A Southern Pines Guide

By the Southern Pines Water Damage Pros team

A leak that soaks a mattress, sofa or chair raises a tough question: dry it or discard it? The answer depends on the water, the item and how fast you act. This guide gives a clear way to decide, and what to photograph so the loss is documented.

Step one: identify the water

Clean water from a supply line, a rain leak or a spilled tub is a very different case from sewage or flood water. Contaminated water gets into foam, padding and fabric, and these cannot be cleaned in place. If the water came from a toilet, a drain or the ground, discard the soft item.

Step two: think about the material

ItemClean water, caught within a dayContaminated water
Mattress, memory foamOften cannot be saved, foam holds waterDiscard
Mattress, innerspringSometimes dried if only the surface got wetDiscard
Upholstered sofa or chairSometimes dried and cleaned if the frame is dryDiscard
Leather furnitureCan sometimes be dried and conditionedDiscard cushions, clean frame
Solid wood frameUsually dried and cleanedCleaned and sanitized if possible
Pillows and cushionsWash on hot or discard if soaked throughDiscard

Foam and padding are the hard part. They trap water deep inside and take a long time to dry, and the interior can sit wet while the surface feels dry. That is where odor and growth start.

What to do right away

  1. Get the item off wet carpet or flooring, onto a hard surface or foil.
  2. Stand it up or prop it so air reaches all sides.
  3. Blot with towels and press out water. Do not rub.
  4. Use fans and a dehumidifier in the room.
  5. Photograph the item and write down its age and value for insurance.
  6. If the item has not dried in a day or two, plan to discard it.

Do not use a hair dryer or heater to speed drying on foam. Excess heat can damage the material and the item may still hold moisture inside. Use moving air and a dehumidifier.

Checking if an item is dry

Press into the middle of a cushion or mattress. If you feel coolness or dampness, it is not dry. A musty smell after drying means moisture is still inside. A moisture meter can read moisture content in wood frames, and thermal imaging shows damp spots in upholstery.

What to document for insurance

The NC Department of Insurance advises homeowners to photograph and list damage before any temporary repairs and to keep receipts. For contents, list each item with its age, price and a photo. Keep a damaged item until the insurer has seen it, if the claim requires it, and ask your insurer how it values contents.

Contents claims often depend on whether the water was sudden and accidental. For how the claim question works, our blog covers general rules, and for drying a room after a leak see structural drying and carpet water damage restoration.

How professionals handle contents

When a crew arrives at a wet house, contents are usually sorted into three groups: items that can stay and dry in place, items that can be moved and cleaned, and items that cannot be saved. Hard goods like solid wood furniture, glass and metal are good candidates for cleaning. Porous soft goods that took on contaminated water are not.

Questions to ask about each item

  1. What kind of water touched it, and for how long?
  2. Is it porous, like foam and fabric, or hard, like wood and metal?
  3. Does it have sentimental or high replacement value that justifies professional cleaning?
  4. Can it be moved to a dry place now without causing more damage?
  5. Does your insurer want to see it before it is thrown out?

Photographs and a written list are the easiest way to hold that decision for later. A bed that you replace next week can still be part of a claim if you have proof of what it was.

Storing items you keep

Once dry, store salvaged items in a clean, dry and ventilated space. Do not wrap damp items in plastic. Check them again in a few days for any musty smell, and do not return them to a room until its readings are normal.

If a child's bed or a family heirloom is involved, say so when you call. Sentimental value can change what is worth a professional cleaning attempt.

Keep the tags and model numbers if you can, because they help an insurer price a replacement.

Local record-keeping note

The NC Department of Insurance says that if a home is uninhabitable, receipts for temporary living expenses such as hotels and meals are worth keeping, and that additional living expense coverage may reimburse them, subject to your policy limits. Document first, then discard.

Source type: NC Department of Insurance claims guidance. Homeowners in Aberdeen and Carthage can use the same records.

Frequently asked questions

Can a wet mattress be saved?

Sometimes an innerspring mattress that got only surface water can be dried, but foam mattresses usually cannot because water settles deep in the foam.

Should I throw away furniture that touched flood water?

Soft items, yes. Flood and sewage water are contaminated and cannot be safely cleaned out of padding and fabric.

How long can a sofa stay wet before it is ruined?

Within a day or two is the limit for clean water. Beyond that, odor and growth risk rise.

Do I need to keep the item for insurance?

Ask your insurer. Photograph it and list it, and keep it if the claim requires an inspection.

Is a hair dryer okay for drying upholstery?

No. Heat can damage foam and glue. Use moving air and a dehumidifier.
