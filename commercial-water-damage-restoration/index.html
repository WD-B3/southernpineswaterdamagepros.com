Emergency Commercial Water Damage in Southern Pines, NC

Every hour a business is wet is an hour it may be closed. Here is how commercial water losses are handled.

☎ Call (910) 387-8176 · 24/7

Emergency commercial water damage restoration in Southern Pines, NC covers stores, offices, restaurants, medical suites, multi-unit buildings and warehouses where a water loss threatens operations. The work is the same science as residential restoration, with extraction and drying, but the priorities differ, because the building is also a business. Our emergency line is open 24/7, including nights and weekends when many commercial losses are discovered.

If you are on site, protect people first. Shut off the water at the main valve if a line is leaking, move inventory and equipment off the floor, and photograph everything. Do not run electronics that got wet. If the loss involves a sprinkler or fire line, call the fire department before anything else.

When a commercial water loss is an emergency

A leak found Monday morning after sitting all weekend has already soaked ceiling tile, carpet, drywall and stock. A burst line above a ceiling can drop water onto servers and inventory. Treat any active water as an emergency. Every day of drying delay adds demolition and downtime.

Power, tenants and shared systems

In multi-tenant buildings water can reach other suites, panels and elevators. Keep people out of wet areas near electrical gear and call the utility or 911 for any electrical hazard. Notify the building owner and any neighboring tenants at once.

What flooding has done to local businesses

During the Chantal flooding in 2025, a shopping center on Brucewood Road took on water, a major retailer was evacuated and a restaurant stayed closed for several weeks, according to a Carolina Public Press report. For a business owner, the lesson is practical. How fast drying starts often decides how fast the doors reopen.

How commercial jobs are run

  1. Stabilize: stop the water, extract and protect contents.
  2. Assess: moisture mapping across walls, slabs, ceilings and flooring.
  3. Plan: a written scope and price explained before work starts.
  4. Dry: air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the building volume.
  5. Document: photographs, logs and moisture readings for the insurer.
  6. Restore: replace materials and coordinate with other contractors.

Large buildings need more equipment and more electrical capacity, and drying plans often phase the work so part of the building can stay open. After-hours work is common. Ceiling tile, insulation, carpet tile and drywall are the most common materials removed, while concrete slabs may need extended drying.

Documentation for commercial claims

Commercial policies can involve business interruption, tenant and landlord responsibilities and lease terms. Photographs, moisture logs, equipment records and itemized scopes help an adjuster understand the loss. Keep receipts for emergency steps and inventory lists. Ask your insurer which documents they want, and ask us to keep a log of each visit.

Commercial cleanup cost factors

Commercial pricing follows the building and the downtime:

  • Square footage and the volume of air being dried.
  • Water category and source.
  • Materials, including ceiling tile, carpet tile, drywall and slab.
  • After-hours or phased work to keep part of the business open.
  • Contents, inventory and equipment that must be moved or protected.

Commercial policies differ widely on water damage, flood, sewer backup and business interruption. Read your policy and ask your insurer, or your broker, before work begins. Do not assume coverage either way.

Records a business should keep

Keep a log of the date and time the damage was found, photos of affected inventory and equipment, and records of any days the business was closed. Your insurer may ask for these when it reviews a business interruption claim.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle commercial water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Southern Pines?

Yes. Calls come through at any hour of any day, including weekends and holidays when many commercial losses are found. Shut off the water, protect people and call.

Can you work while my business stays open?

Often partly. A phased plan can dry one area while another operates, depending on safety, noise and access. We explain the plan before work starts.

What documentation should I keep for a commercial claim?

Photographs, moisture readings, equipment logs, receipts and inventory lists. Ask your insurer or broker what they require.

Do you handle multi-unit buildings?

Yes. Multi-unit losses require coordination with owners, tenants and sometimes other contractors, and the plan covers shared walls and ceilings.

Does commercial insurance cover water damage?

It depends on the policy and on the cause. Business interruption, flood and sewer backup are often separate. Ask your insurer or broker.
