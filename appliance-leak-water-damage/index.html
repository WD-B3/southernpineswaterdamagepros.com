Emergency Appliance Leak Water Damage in Southern Pines, NC

Appliance leaks start small and spread quietly. Learn where they come from and how the drying works.

☎ Call (910) 387-8176 · 24/7

Emergency appliance leak water damage in Southern Pines, NC usually begins with a washing machine hose, a dishwasher supply line, a refrigerator ice maker line or a drain that backs up. Appliance leak water damage is the damage from water released by a machine, as opposed to the pipes in the wall. Our emergency line is open 24/7.

Unplug the appliance if you can reach the outlet without stepping in water, and close the shutoff valve behind or beneath it. Most washers and dishwashers have their own small valve on the supply line. Mop up what you can, and photograph the area before drying equipment goes in.

When an appliance leak is an emergency

If water is running or pooling, it is an emergency. A burst washer hose can release many gallons while you are at work. A dishwasher leak can seep under cabinets and into a subfloor before it shows at the baseboard. Refrigerator lines tend to leak quietly behind the unit.

Electricity and appliances

Do not unplug a machine while standing in water. If the outlet is wet or the appliance is sparking, leave it and cut power at the panel only if the panel is dry. Call 911 for any electrical fire risk before calling us.

Common appliance leak sources

  • Washing machine hoses, which can burst or loosen at the faucet connection.
  • Dishwasher supply lines and door seals.
  • Refrigerator and freezer ice maker lines, and clogged defrost drains.
  • Water heater fittings, covered separately on our water heater page.
  • Drain hoses and overflowing laundry standpipes.

Many appliance leaks begin at flexible supply hoses. If yours are older, check them for bulging or cracks. Replacing a worn hose costs far less than cleaning up after one fails.

How the water spreads

Appliances sit against cabinets and walls, and water follows the path of least resistance. Under a dishwasher, water moves along the subfloor beneath cabinets. Behind a refrigerator, it travels along the baseboard and into the neighboring room. Laundry rooms upstairs send water through the ceiling below.

That is why surface cleanup is not enough. Moisture meters and thermal imaging find water in cabinet bases, toe-kicks, subfloor and wall cavities. Drying starts there, using air movers and dehumidifiers, and cabinets are removed only when drying in place will not work.

Cabinets, floors and the rest of the kitchen

Water under the dishwasher often involves cabinet bases and flooring. Our page on kitchen water damage restoration explains what happens to cabinets and the sink area. If the floor underneath is wood or laminate, see hardwood floor water damage repair.

Appliance leak cleanup cost factors

An appliance leak is priced by what the water reached:

  • How long the leak ran before it was found.
  • Floor type, from tile and vinyl to wood and carpet.
  • Cabinet bases and walls that soaked up water.
  • Whether the leak reached a lower floor.
  • Drying days and equipment.

Sudden appliance failures are often covered as accidental water damage, while repeated slow leaks and wear are often excluded. Your policy may also treat the appliance itself differently from the damage it caused. Check your policy and ask your insurer.

After the appliance is fixed

Once a repair technician has replaced the hose, valve or pump, ask us to check the floor, cabinet base and wall behind the appliance. Moisture that remains there can cause swelling and odors weeks later.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle appliance leak emergencies at night or on weekends in Southern Pines?

Yes. Day or night, the phone is answered. Close the appliance's shutoff valve and unplug it if that is safe, then call.

Which appliance leaks cause the most damage?

Washing machine hoses and dishwasher supply lines tend to release the most water because they are under household pressure. Refrigerator lines leak less but can go unnoticed for months.

Should I move the appliance myself?

Be careful. Moving a wet appliance can strain hoses and electrical cords. If water is under it, disconnect and shut off the supply first, then ask a crew to move it.

Is the appliance covered by insurance?

The appliance and the damage it caused may be treated differently. Many policies cover sudden water damage but not the appliance itself. Ask your insurer.

How can I prevent another leak?

Inspect supply hoses, replace worn ones, keep drain pans clear and know where each shutoff valve is. A leak sensor on the floor is a low-cost warning.
