Prevent Water Damage While Away From Your Southern Pines Home

By the Southern Pines Water Damage Pros team

Many Southern Pines owners travel, winter elsewhere or keep a second home. A leak in an empty house can run for days before anyone knows. This checklist covers the steps that matter most before you leave.

Before you leave

  1. Close the main water valve for any trip longer than a few days.
  2. Open a faucet to drain pressure, then close it.
  3. Turn the water heater to vacation or off, following its manual.
  4. Check supply hoses on the washer, dishwasher and ice maker.
  5. Clean gutters and make sure downspouts drain away from the house.
  6. Set the thermostat to a safe range, and keep the air conditioner drain clear.
  7. Ask a neighbor or friend to check the house on a schedule.

Smart devices that help

  • Water leak sensors near the water heater, sinks and washer.
  • A smart shutoff valve that closes the main when it detects a leak.
  • A sump pump alarm or battery backup.
  • A temperature sensor that alerts you to a freeze or a failed air conditioner.

Sensors only help if the alerts reach someone. Make sure notifications go to your phone and to a second person who can enter the house. A device that sounds in an empty house does nothing.

Power outages and storms

Storms can cut power while you are gone. When Tropical Depression Chantal hit Moore County in 2025, thousands of customers lost power in the storm and its aftermath. Without power, a sump pump stops and an air conditioner stops, and food and humidity become problems at once. A battery backup for a sump pump and an alert for lost power are worth the effort.

When you get back or get a call

Close the main if water is running, stay out of any room with standing water near electrical outlets and photograph everything before cleanup. Then call your insurer and a restoration crew. For standing water see water extraction, and for hurricane or storm damage see storm water damage cleanup.

Second homes and seasonal owners

Many Sandhills homes sit empty for weeks while owners are traveling or living elsewhere for part of the year. Empty houses are riskier than lived-in ones because no one notices a problem for days. A small leak that would be caught in an hour in an occupied home can run for a month in an empty one.

A leave-the-house routine

  • Walk through the house, looking under sinks and around appliances.
  • Close the main valve and drain a faucet or two.
  • Set the thermostat and confirm the air conditioner drain is clear.
  • Take photos of each room as your before record.
  • Leave a key and your contact details with a neighbor or property manager.
  • Tell your insurer if the house will be vacant for an extended period, since some policies have conditions about vacancy.

That last point is easy to miss. Some policies limit coverage after a house has been unoccupied for a set period, so read the vacancy language before a long trip.

Check in on a schedule

A weekly visit from a neighbor, family member or property manager catches most problems early. Ask them to run a faucet, flush a toilet, look at ceilings and check the floor around the water heater. Give them your plumber's number and permission to call, and make sure they know where the main valve is.

What to tell your sitter

  • Where the main valve is and how to close it.
  • Which plumber, electrician and restoration company to call.
  • That a small puddle is worth a phone call right away.
  • How to reach you and a second contact.
  • Not to enter a room with standing water if the power is on.

If you rent the house out

Short-term guests do not know where the main valve is. Put the location in the house guide, and consider smart sensors under sinks and near the water heater so you hear about problems before the next guest does.

Review the plan each year before your first long trip, since sensors age, batteries die and neighbors move away.

After a long absence

When you return, walk through before unpacking. Smell each room, check ceilings and cabinets, and run each faucet for a minute while you look underneath. Open the main valve slowly, then recheck the meter and fixtures for any movement before you settle in.

Source-checked local notes

Carolina Public Press reported thousands without power in Moore County after Chantal in 2025. The Town of Southern Pines Utilities Division posts closures and utility work on its Road Closures page, which is useful to check from afar. Ask a neighbor to confirm conditions at your address.

Frequently asked questions

Should I shut off the water when I travel?

Yes, for trips of more than a few days. It removes the risk of a hose or line failing while you are away.

Are smart leak sensors worth it?

They can be, if alerts reach someone who can act. Pair them with a neighbor who has a key.

What happens to a sump pump in a power outage?

It stops. A battery backup keeps it running.

What should I do if I return to a flooded house?

Close the main, stay away from electrical hazards, photograph the damage and call your insurer and a restoration crew.

Can I leave the air conditioner off while away?

In summer humidity can build, so many owners set a higher temperature instead of turning it off.
