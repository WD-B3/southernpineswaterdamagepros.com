How to Prepare Your Southern Pines Home for Storm Season

By the Southern Pines Water Damage Pros team

Most storm damage is cheaper to prevent than to repair. A few hours of checking the roof edges, gutters, drains and power backup before a tropical system arrives can change how much water gets into a house. This checklist is written for Southern Pines homes and is meant to be done well before a storm is in the forecast.

What storms have done in Moore County

In 2025, Tropical Depression Chantal dropped nearly 10 inches of rain on Moore County in a matter of hours. County officials called it the worst flood event in the region since Hurricane Fran in 1996. A dam failed at the Longleaf Golf Course, culverts were overwhelmed, thousands lost power and rescuers made 13 swift water rescues on flooded roads.

The lesson is not that every storm is that large. It is that rain, wind and power loss arrive together, so a home needs protection against all three. Water can enter from above through the roof, from the side through windows and doors, and from below through low spots and failed drainage.

The exterior checklist

  • Roof: look for missing, cracked or lifted shingles, and check flashing around chimneys, vents and skylights.
  • Gutters and downspouts: clear leaves and pine needles, and make sure downspouts discharge several feet away from the foundation.
  • Grading: soil should slope away from the house, and low spots next to the foundation should be filled.
  • Trees: remove dead limbs that could fall on the roof or lines.
  • Windows and doors: check seals and weatherstripping where wind-driven rain pushes in.
  • Outdoor drains and culverts on your property: keep them clear of debris.

Pine straw and leaves are the usual cause of clogged gutters in the Sandhills, and clogged gutters spill water at the roof edge and down the wall. If you find a roof problem, a roofer should handle it. When water has already come in, see our page on storm water damage cleanup.

The interior checklist

  1. Find your main water shutoff valve and make sure it turns.
  2. Test your sump pump if you have one, and consider a battery backup, since power failures often come with storms.
  3. Raise valuables and electronics off basement or ground-level floors.
  4. Check that your water heater and appliances are on stable bases, not directly on the floor of a low area.
  5. Photograph each room and your major belongings for insurance records.
  6. Store important papers in a waterproof container.

Power and utilities

The Town of Southern Pines Utilities Division links to a Road Closures and Utility Work page for scheduled and emergency closures and outages. Check it, and your power company, before and after a storm. Have a plan for losing power, including a way to keep a phone charged and a flashlight at hand.

Insurance before the storm

The NC Department of Insurance states that homeowners policies do not cover flood or rising water, and that flood coverage is a separate policy. Check your coverage before a storm, since new flood policies often have a waiting period before they take effect. Ask your insurer about wind or hurricane deductibles too, and about sewer or drain backup coverage.

After the storm

If water entered the house, protect the opening if it is safe, photograph the damage before temporary repairs and keep receipts. Hold off on permanent repairs until your insurer has inspected. For rising water, see flood damage cleanup.

Making a household plan

A storm plan works best when everyone in the house knows it. Agree on where the water shutoff is, who calls whom, and where important papers are kept. Save your insurance policy number and the number of a trusted plumber and electrician in your phone, and keep a paper copy in a waterproof bag in case your phone dies.

Frequently asked questions

When should I start preparing my Southern Pines home for storm season?

Well ahead of any forecast. Gutter cleaning, roof checks and tree trimming are easier before a storm is in the forecast.

Do I need flood insurance in Southern Pines?

Homeowners policies do not cover flood or rising water, according to the NC Department of Insurance. Check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask your insurer or agent about a separate flood policy.

What should I photograph before a storm?

Each room, major belongings, the roof and the exterior. Do it before a storm so you have a before record.

Should I turn off the water before a storm?

Not usually, but know where the main valve is. If you evacuate or leave for days, shutting it off can prevent a burst line from flooding the house.

What happened in Moore County during Tropical Depression Chantal?

In 2025 it brought heavy rain, a dam at the Longleaf Golf Course failed, culverts were overwhelmed and thousands lost power, according to Carolina Public Press.
