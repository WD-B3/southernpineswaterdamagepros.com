How to Find a Hidden Water Leak in Your Southern Pines Home

By the Southern Pines Water Damage Pros team

A hidden leak can run for weeks before anything shows. By the time you see a stain, the structure may already be wet. This guide covers simple checks that find leaks early, from the water meter to bill history.

Do the meter test

  1. Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water, including the ice maker.
  2. Find your water meter and note the reading or watch the small leak indicator.
  3. Wait an hour without using water, then check again.
  4. If the reading moved or the indicator spins, water is leaking somewhere.

If it moved, close the main valve and check again. If it stops moving, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak may be between the meter and the house.

Check your billing history

A steady rise in your water bill with no change in habits is a classic sign of a hidden leak. The Town of Southern Pines Utilities Division provides billing and account information, so compare recent bills to the same months last year. Watch for sudden jumps.

Walk the house

  • Listen at night for hissing or dripping in walls.
  • Look under sinks, around toilets and behind appliances.
  • Check ceilings below bathrooms for stains.
  • Feel baseboards and walls for cool or damp spots.
  • Look for warm spots on a slab floor, which can suggest a hot water line leak.
  • Check the yard for soggy or unusually green spots near the line.

When to call a professional

Call a plumber for a leak you cannot find or fix. Call a restoration crew when water has soaked materials. A thermal camera and moisture meter can map wet areas without opening walls, which saves demolition. Do not cut into walls before you know the source.

Sounds, smells and small signs

Hidden leaks give off small clues long before they stain a wall. A faint hissing near a water heater, the sound of water moving when nothing is running, a toilet that refills by itself, or a water meter that creeps forward overnight are all signs worth checking. Smells matter too, especially a musty smell in a closet or under a sink that never seems to go away.

A fifteen minute leak walk

  1. Start at the water heater and look for rust, drips and a damp floor.
  2. Check each toilet base and supply line for moisture.
  3. Open every sink cabinet and run a dry paper towel along the pipes.
  4. Look at the washer hoses and the wall behind the washer.
  5. Check the ceiling below each bathroom and the wall beside each shower.
  6. End outside, looking at spigots, irrigation valves and the soil near the foundation.

Leaks under a concrete slab are the hardest to find. Warm spots on a floor, a bill that climbs without a reason or the sound of running water under the floor are the classic signs. A licensed plumber can use listening equipment to pinpoint these, and a moisture check can map what is wet.

Fix the source, then dry the structure

Finding the leak is only half the job. Once the plumber repairs the line, the wet materials still need to be dried, and a drying plan should be set up before walls and floors are closed back up. Skipping that step is how a repaired leak becomes a mold problem months later.

When a leak is not in the house

If the meter keeps moving after the main valve is closed, the leak may be on the line between the meter and the house. Soggy ground, a patch of unusually green grass or a drop in water pressure can all point to a buried line. That repair belongs to a plumber or a utility contractor, and it is worth calling the Town's Utilities Division to ask who is responsible for which part of the line.

Keep a note of your meter reading on the first of each month. A simple log shows a slow trend long before a bill does, and it gives a plumber a head start when you call.

Source-checked local notes

The Town of Southern Pines Utilities Division serves more than 11,500 water connections and handles account questions on its Water and Sewer pages. Its billing history is a free way to spot a rising pattern, though a bill alone cannot locate a leak. If your jump is large and sudden, ask the Division whether it can check the meter.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find a hidden water leak?

Run a meter test with all fixtures off, check your bill history, and look at sinks, toilets, water heaters and ceilings.

Can a high water bill mean a leak?

It can. A steady or sudden rise without a change in habits is a common sign.

How do I test a toilet for a leak?

Add food coloring to the tank and wait. Color in the bowl without flushing means a leak.

Do I need to open walls to find a leak?

Not always. Moisture meters and thermal cameras can often map wet areas first.

Who do I call for a hidden leak?

A plumber for the repair and a restoration crew for drying.
