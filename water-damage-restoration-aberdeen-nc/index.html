When water is spreading in Aberdeen

If water is moving across a floor or through a ceiling, treat it as an emergency. Shut off the main water valve, lift valuables, and keep out of standing water near outlets. Then call us. The line is answered around the clock, and we explain the plan and price before any work begins.

Stop the source

Close the main valve or the fixture valve. Open a low faucet to drain the line.

Make it safe

Switch off power to the affected rooms if the panel is in a dry spot you can reach. Stay out from under sagging ceilings.

Protect belongings

Raise furniture legs onto foil or plastic and move electronics up. Photograph the damage first.

How water reaches Aberdeen homes

Aberdeen Creek flows south through the middle of town and joins Drowning Creek, the upper name of the Lumber River. Town records show it covers about 11 square miles. Homes along the creek and in low spots take storm runoff first, so check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address.

Most water losses are not floods. Supply lines, water heaters and appliances cause the majority of calls in any town. For pipe failures see burst pipe water damage, and for a standing-water emergency see water extraction. If the water rose from outside after heavy rain, flood damage cleanup explains how contaminated water is handled.

Older housing and the railroad town

Scottish emigrants settled the area beginning in 1745, a post office opened in 1877 under the name Blue's Crossing, and the town was renamed Aberdeen in 1888 and incorporated in 1893. The Aberdeen Historic District, the John Blue House and the Malcolm Blue Farm are all on the National Register of Historic Places. Older homes near the historic center may have original wood floors and older plumbing, so tell us the age of your home when you call.

What we do after you call

A crew stops or confirms the source, extracts standing water and maps moisture with meters and thermal imaging. Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry the structure, and readings are logged each visit. If the water came through a ceiling, see ceiling water damage repair. Neighboring towns are covered by our pages for Pinehurst and Pinebluff.

Give your street, the nearest main road and whether the water is still running. Mention the age of the house if you know it, since older plumbing and crawl spaces change how we approach the job.

Frequently asked questions

Is emergency water removal available in Aberdeen?

Yes. Our dispatch line answers around the clock, and it takes calls from Aberdeen. We explain scope and price before work starts.

Do you cover every ZIP code in Aberdeen?

Yes. Aberdeen uses ZIP 28315, and that is the ZIP we cover for the town, from the Sandhills Boulevard corridor to streets along Aberdeen Creek.

How do I know if my Aberdeen home is in a flood zone?

Use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and enter your address. Ask the Town of Aberdeen about local drainage and any permit requirements for repairs.

Does Aberdeen Creek flood homes?

Creeks can rise during heavy rain, and low spots take water first. We cannot tell you whether a specific address floods, so check the FEMA map and your insurance policy.

Will homeowners insurance cover creek flooding?

Usually not. The NC Department of Insurance says homeowners policies do not cover flood or rising water, and flood coverage is a separate policy.
