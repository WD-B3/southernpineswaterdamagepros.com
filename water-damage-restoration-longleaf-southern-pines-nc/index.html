Water Damage Restoration in Longleaf, Southern Pines, NC

Flood cleanup and drying around the Longleaf golf community.

☎ Call (910) 387-8176 · 24/7

Longleaf is a residential community built around the Longleaf golf course in Southern Pines. It is the one neighborhood in our research where the Town itself documented a storm event: after Tropical Storm Chantal on July 6, 2025, the Town of Southern Pines reported that a dam breach at the Longleaf golf course lake damaged nearby streets and homes.

What the Town reported

The Town's releases listed closures at Hunter Trail at Lakeside Court, Knoll Road North at Hunter Trail and Knoll Road North at Magnolia Circle. A later Town release described widespread flooding and road damage in the Longleaf neighborhood and said the street network kept residents able to reach their homes. It also listed sanitary sewer overflows, including one tied to the dam failure at Longleaf Country Club, and classified the storm as a 500 to 1,000 year flood event, with 8 to 10 inches of rain in a few hours.

Those are documented facts about one event. They do not mean every home in Longleaf flooded, and they do not mean the neighborhood will flood again. No FEMA zone finding was confirmed for the community as a whole, because zones can differ from one lot to the next.

About the homes

Real estate directories describe a mix of single family homes and attached homes, with listed build years from 1989 to 2019. Those directories are third party summaries, so treat the dates as approximate. We make no claim about basements, crawl spaces or plumbing materials in the community.

Why flood water needs careful handling

Water that enters from outside, or that mixes with a sewer system, is treated as contaminated. It can carry bacteria and debris, and it cannot be dried out with household fans or cleaned with a regular vacuum. Porous materials such as carpet pad, insulation and some drywall are often removed. See flood damage restoration and sewage backup cleanup for how those jobs are planned.

  • Keep people and pets out of standing water and mud.
  • Do not enter flooded rooms if outlets, cords or appliances may be live.
  • Do not touch or move suspected hazardous materials.
  • Document the high water line, damaged items and the source with photos.
  • Call the Town or your utility about immediate dangers such as downed lines or road closures.

Drying and records

After water is removed, wet structure is dried with dehumidifiers and air movers and checked with moisture readings. See water extraction and structural drying for the steps. Ask your insurer about coverage first. The NC Department of Insurance states that a standard homeowners policy excludes flood and rising water, which need their own policy. Keep photos, receipts and notes either way.

Frequently asked questions

Did Longleaf flood during Tropical Storm Chantal?

The Town reported that a dam breach at the Longleaf golf course lake damaged nearby streets and homes. It did not publish a count of affected houses.

Is Longleaf in a FEMA flood zone?

Not confirmed for the community as a whole. FEMA maps are checked property by property, so look up your own lot.

Is flood water safe to clean myself?

Not safely. Flood and sewer water can be contaminated, so call a professional and keep people and pets away.

Can carpet and drywall be saved?

Sometimes for clean water caught quickly. Contaminated water usually means porous materials are removed.

Does homeowners insurance cover flood?

Standard policies exclude it, per the NC Department of Insurance, so flood needs a separate policy. Confirm with your insurer.
