How to Shut Off Your Home Water Main in Southern Pines, NC

By the Southern Pines Water Damage Pros team

Knowing how to shut off the water can save thousands of dollars in damage. When a pipe bursts, every minute counts, and fumbling around for a valve you have never touched is the slowest way to find it. Find it now, test it once and mark it so anyone in the house can use it.

Two shutoffs, two responsibilities

Most homes have two places to stop the water. The first is the main valve on the house side, usually inside or at the outside wall, which you are responsible for operating. The second is the meter shutoff near the street, which belongs to the utility. The Town of Southern Pines Utilities Division provides water distribution to more than 11,500 service connections, so ask the Town before operating anything at the meter.

Where to look

  • Where the water line enters the house, often in a utility room, garage or basement.
  • Near the water heater, which is usually close to the main.
  • In a crawl space near the front wall.
  • Under a cover or in a small box in the yard near the meter.
  • Inside a closet or cabinet near the front of the house.

Follow the pipe from the water heater toward the front of the house, and look for a round wheel handle or a lever. Take a photo and label the valve with a tag so a guest or family member can find it.

Types of main valves

Valve typeHow it looksHow to close it
Gate valveRound wheel handleTurn clockwise several full turns until firm
Ball valveLever handleTurn the lever a quarter turn so it sits perpendicular to the pipe
Curb or meter valveUnderground, with a cap or boxUse only if the utility allows, with the correct key

Gate valves, especially old ones, can stick or break. Test yours once a year by closing it fully and opening it again. If it will not move, ask a plumber to replace it before an emergency.

What to do when you shut it off

  1. Close the main valve fully.
  2. Open a faucet at the lowest point in the house to drain remaining pressure and water.
  3. Turn off the water heater power or gas if the heater will be dry.
  4. Check for the source of the leak, but do not go near electrical hazards.
  5. Photograph damage before you move things.
  6. Call a plumber for the repair and a restoration crew for drying if water spread.

Shutting off the water stops new water, but it does not remove what is already in your house. For that, see water extraction, and for supply-line failures our page on burst pipe water damage.

When the water must stay off

Leaving the house for several days is a good time to close the main valve. A failed supply line while you are away can run until you return. Washer hoses and ice maker lines are common failure points, and shutting off the water removes the risk.

Practice makes the real thing easy

Once a year, close your main valve fully, open a faucet to confirm the water stops, then open the valve again. The test takes ten minutes and tells you whether the valve works. A valve that has not moved in years can seize, and the first time you discover it in an emergency is the worst time.

Make it findable

  • Attach a bright tag to the valve handle.
  • Tape a card nearby that says which way to turn and how many turns it takes.
  • Keep a clear path to the valve, free of boxes and stored items.
  • Show every adult in the household, and any house sitter, where it is.
  • Write the valve location in the notes you give a pet sitter or house guest.

Also learn where your appliance shutoffs are. Toilets, sinks and washing machines usually have small stop valves of their own, and closing one of those can stop a local leak without cutting water to the whole house.

If your main valve is buried or hard to reach, ask a plumber about installing an accessible shutoff. It is a small project compared with the damage a single burst line can cause.

Southern Pines utility detail

The Town of Southern Pines Utilities Division lists regular hours of Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., and a separate emergency number on its Water and Sewer page. If you cannot close a valve, or you suspect a break on the Town side, use that emergency line. The Division also says that sewer obstructions between the property line and the sewer main are the customer's responsibility unless roots in the right-of-way or pipe failure are the cause.

Source type: Town of Southern Pines Utilities Division page. Homes in Pinebluff or Sanford are served by different utilities, so check yours.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the main water shutoff in most homes?

Where the water line enters the house, often near the water heater, in a utility room, garage, basement or crawl space.

Is it okay to use the meter shutoff at the street?

Ask your utility first. In Southern Pines, ask the Town's Utilities Division before operating anything at the meter.

What if the valve will not turn?

Do not force it. Call a plumber, and use the utility's emergency line if the leak is serious.

Should I shut off the water when I travel?

Yes, for trips of more than a few days. It removes the risk of a hose or supply line failing while you are away.

Who do I call in Southern Pines if I cannot stop a leak?

The Town's Utilities Division lists an emergency number on its Water and Sewer page. A plumber can also help with the house side.
