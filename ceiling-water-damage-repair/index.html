Emergency Ceiling Water Damage Repair in Southern Pines, NC

A wet ceiling can fail without warning. Learn what to do first, what causes it and how the repair works.

☎ Call (910) 387-8176 · 24/7

Emergency ceiling water damage repair in Southern Pines, NC covers the finished ceiling and everything above it, whatever the source. A leaking roof, an upstairs bathroom, a supply line or an air handler in the attic can all soak the same sheet of drywall. Our emergency line is open 24/7, and a dripping or sagging ceiling is exactly the kind of call it is for.

Move people and furniture out from under any bulge. Place a bucket under active drips, and if the ceiling is holding water, ask a crew about relieving it safely instead of poking it yourself. If the source is a plumbing line above, close the main valve. Photograph the stain before anything is repaired.

When a wet ceiling is an emergency

Sagging, bulging or dripping

A ceiling that is sagging holds weight in water and insulation, and it can collapse. Keep out from under it. Do not turn on lights or fans in a wet ceiling, and if you see sparks or smell burning, leave and call 911 first.

A flat brown stain with no sag is less urgent than an active drip, but it still needs attention the same day, because the water source is still unknown. Stains that return after drying mean moisture is still arriving from somewhere.

Finding where the water comes from

Water travels along rafters and pipes before it drops, so the stain is often not under the leak. A roof leak may enter at a flashing or vent boot and run down a rafter for several feet. A supply line leak tends to produce a steady stain that grows with every use. A condensate line or air handler drain can drip only when the system runs.

Thermal imaging and moisture meters help find the wet area without cutting more than needed. The attic matters as much as the ceiling, because wet insulation above the stain holds water long after the leak stops.

Roof leaks and what they do to the ceiling

This page also covers roof leaks, because a roof leak shows up as a ceiling problem first. Rain finds a gap in shingles, flashing, or around a chimney or vent, soaks the decking and insulation, and then stains the drywall below. The roofer fixes the roof, and we handle the water inside the house.

  • Dry or replace saturated attic insulation that has lost its value.
  • Check the roof deck and rafters for wet wood, since drywall can hide a larger problem.
  • Open the ceiling where drying in place is not possible.
  • Dry the cavity with air movers before any new drywall goes up.

After storms, such as the heavy rain that hit Moore County with Tropical Depression Chantal in 2025, ceiling stains often appear days later. That gap is why it helps to check ceilings after any major storm, not only when water is visible. See also our page on storm water damage cleanup.

How the ceiling is repaired

Drywall that has sagged, softened or crumbled is cut out and replaced. Drywall that is only stained but still firm can sometimes be dried and sealed with a stain-blocking primer before paint. Texture matching comes last, and it is the step that decides whether the repair is invisible. Painting before the cavity is dry is the most common reason stains return.

Ceiling repair cost factors

The repair price follows the scope of the damage, not just the stain size:

  • Whether the drywall can dry in place or must be replaced.
  • Wet insulation above the ceiling and how much of it.
  • Ceiling type, including texture, height and access.
  • Number of rooms affected and whether paint is needed beyond the repair area.
  • The source of the leak, because it decides whether the water will keep coming.

Sudden water from a storm-damaged roof or a burst pipe is often covered, but long-running leaks and lack of maintenance often are not. Ask your insurer, keep photographs of the stain and the roof, and hold off on permanent repairs until the insurer has had a chance to look. The NC Department of Insurance advises documenting damage before temporary repairs.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle ceiling water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Southern Pines?

Yes. Weekend and overnight calls are normal for us. If the ceiling is dripping or sagging, keep out from under it, close the main valve if a plumbing line is the source, and call.

Is a ceiling stain always a roof leak?

No. Plumbing lines, bathrooms above, and HVAC drains can all stain a ceiling. A stain that grows after rain points to the roof, and one that grows with water use points to plumbing.

Can a sagging ceiling be saved?

Rarely. Drywall that has sagged with water usually has to come down. Relieving the water in a controlled way prevents a larger collapse.

Do you fix the roof?

We handle the water damage inside the house. A roofer fixes the roof itself. We coordinate with whichever roofer you hire so the ceiling is not repaired until the source is stopped.

Why did the stain come back after painting?

Usually the cavity was not dry, or the source kept leaking. Stain-blocking primer helps, but it cannot fix an active leak.
