Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage in Southern Pines?

By the Southern Pines Water Damage Pros team

Most homeowners ask the same first question after a leak: will insurance pay for this? The honest answer is that it depends on the policy, the cause of the water and how the loss unfolded. This guide explains the general pattern so you know what to look for in your own policy, and what to ask your insurer.

The short version: sudden versus gradual

Homeowners policies tend to draw a line between sudden and accidental water damage and damage from long-term leaks, wear or neglect. A supply line that bursts overnight is the classic covered event. A pipe that dripped under a sink for six months is the classic exclusion. Insurers read that difference closely.

The same pattern applies to appliances. A washing machine hose that fails and floods a laundry room is usually treated as sudden. A refrigerator line that seeped for a year behind the unit often is not. Your policy language decides, so read the water damage section before you need it.

What is usually not covered

  • Flood and rising water. The NC Department of Insurance states that homeowners policies do not cover flood or rising water, and that flood coverage is bought separately, often through the National Flood Insurance Program.
  • Sewer or drain backup, unless a specific endorsement was added.
  • Long-term seepage, rot, and damage from poor maintenance.
  • Groundwater coming through basement walls or floors, which is often treated like flood.

Many homeowners are surprised by the sewer backup exclusion. Check your policy for the words sewer, drain or sump pump. If you do not find them, ask your insurer whether an endorsement exists and what it costs.

Steps that protect a claim

  1. Stop the damage. Shut off the water and make emergency protective repairs. Policies generally expect you to limit further loss.
  2. Photograph and list the damage before any temporary repairs, as the NC Department of Insurance advises.
  3. Keep receipts for emergency repairs and, if the house is uninhabitable, for living expenses. Your policy may include additional living expense coverage, so ask about its limits.
  4. Hold off on permanent repairs until your insurer has inspected and you have agreed on the scope.
  5. Keep a log of calls, claim numbers and names.

Drying logs and moisture readings from a restoration company can help document the loss. For what a professional drying record looks like, see structural drying. For burst lines specifically, our page on burst pipe water damage covers the cleanup side.

Questions worth asking your insurer

  • Is this loss treated as sudden and accidental?
  • What is my deductible for water damage, and is there a separate one for wind or storms?
  • Does my policy include sewer or drain backup?
  • Will you cover tearing out and replacing flooring, cabinets or drywall, and how is matching handled?
  • How long do I have to file, and what documents do you need?

The NC Department of Insurance notes that its claims guidance does not state a single filing deadline, and tells homeowners to contact their insurer or the Department to confirm time limits. Do not wait to ask.

A practical way to read your own policy

Sit down with the declarations page and the water damage section before a loss, not after. Look for three things: what the policy calls a covered cause, what it excludes by name, and what the deductible is. Then ask your agent two plain questions in writing, one about sudden pipe failures and one about gradual seepage, and keep the answers with your policy papers.

Many owners are surprised to learn that coverage can turn on timing. A supply line that splits overnight is treated differently from a slow drip under a sink that went unnoticed for months. Neither answer is guaranteed, which is why photographs and dated records are worth more than memory.

What this looks like in Southern Pines

The source of the water often matters more than the amount. The Chantal flooding of 2025 is a local example. Homeowners whose water came up from the ground faced the flood exclusion, while those whose rain came through damaged roofs faced a different set of policy terms.

Source: NC Department of Insurance claims guidance and Carolina Public Press reporting on the 2025 storm. If you live in Pinehurst or Aberdeen, the same policy rules apply. See our pages for Pinehurst and Aberdeen for local service details.

Frequently asked questions

Does homeowners insurance cover a burst pipe in North Carolina?

Often, when the burst was sudden and accidental, but policies differ. Read the water damage section of your policy and ask your insurer.

Is flood damage covered by a standard homeowners policy?

No. The NC Department of Insurance says homeowners policies do not cover flood or rising water. Flood coverage is bought separately, often through the National Flood Insurance Program.

Does insurance cover a slow leak I did not notice?

Often not. Gradual leaks and wear are commonly excluded. Ask your insurer how your policy treats the specific cause.

Is sewer backup covered?

Usually only with an added endorsement. Look for sewer or drain backup language in your policy.

Which Southern Pines storm event changed how locals think about flood coverage?

Tropical Depression Chantal in 2025, which flooded roads and homes across Moore County. Many homeowners learned that rising water falls under flood coverage, not their homeowners policy.
