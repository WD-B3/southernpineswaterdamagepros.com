Water Damage Restoration in Weymouth Heights, Southern Pines, NC

Careful water removal and drying for the historic homes at the south end of Southern Pines.

☎ Call (910) 387-8176 · 24/7

Weymouth Heights sits at the south end of the Southern Pines Historic District, where the street grid gives way to curving lanes beneath tall pines. The National Register nomination for the district describes these curving streets as the product of early 20th century Weymouth Heights subdivisions. The district record dates many of these homes to the early 20th century through the early 1930s, and that age shapes how water damage should be handled.

What the district record says about these homes

The nomination, listed in 1991, covers about 320 acres and 490 contributing buildings. It notes a mix of shingled cottages, Colonial Revival houses, English and Norman cottages, Tudor style houses and a group of masonry houses known as Lachine houses, many of them designed by architects Aymar Embury II and Alfred Yeomans. It also describes the land sloping from wooded hills at the south end down toward US Highway 1.

The record describes architecture, not water history. We are not claiming that Weymouth Heights has a flooding problem or that older homes leak more often. What it tells a restoration crew is what is likely to be inside the walls: plaster, solid wood floors, masonry and mature trees close to the foundation. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified, so look up your own address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.

How water damage is handled in an older home

  • Plaster and lath hold moisture differently from modern drywall, so readings are taken before anything is cut.
  • Solid hardwood can often be dried in place when water is removed quickly, and see hardwood floor water damage repair for how that is judged.
  • Masonry and crawl spaces dry slowly and are checked separately.
  • Original trim and built-ins are protected and documented before work starts.
  • Moisture readings guide when equipment can come out.

Many roof, gutter and downspout problems in tree-covered neighborhoods start with pine needles and leaves, and a slow leak near a chimney or valley can wet a ceiling long before a stain appears. When that happens, see ceiling water damage repair.

What to do while you wait for help

  1. Avoid rooms with standing water when outlets, cords or the panel may be wet.
  2. Shut the main water valve only when it is safe and easy to reach.
  3. Move small valuables, rugs and books away from wet areas.
  4. Record the damage and its source with phone photos and a short video.
  5. Call us and describe what is wet, how long it has been wet and what the house is built from.

Water from a supply line is different from water that came in from outside or from a drain. If the water looks dirty or smells like sewage, keep people and pets out and do not try to dry it with household fans. See water extraction and structural drying for how removal and drying work.

Insurance documentation

Whether a loss is covered depends on the cause and your policy, so talk to your insurer early. Photos, dated notes and moisture readings help describe the damage. They do not guarantee payment. The NC Department of Insurance notes that flood and rising water generally need separate flood coverage.

Frequently asked questions

Do you work on historic homes in Weymouth Heights?

Yes. Homes of any age are fine. Mention the age and building materials on the call so we can plan around plaster, hardwood and masonry.

Is Weymouth Heights in a FEMA flood zone?

That was not verified for the neighborhood. A lookup for your exact address will tell you, and flooding can still happen outside mapped zones.

Can hardwood floors be saved after a leak?

Sometimes, when water is removed quickly and drying is monitored. Cupping and finish damage are judged once readings return to normal.

Do you drill or cut plaster walls?

Only after readings show it is needed, and we explain the scope and price before work starts.

Will insurance cover my loss?

Coverage turns on how the water got in and what your policy says. Your insurer decides, so ask early and save everything in writing.
