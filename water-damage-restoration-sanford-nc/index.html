Water damage restoration in Sanford, NC reaches the Lee County seat, about 26 miles northeast of Southern Pines along US 1. Sanford uses ZIP codes 27330, 27331 and 27332, and our emergency line serves the city around the clock. Sanford is farther than our Moore County towns, so we say so up front and explain what to expect when you call.

Sanford's downtown and older neighborhoods

Sanford was incorporated in 1874, grew as a railroad and industrial center, and became known as the Brick Capital of the USA in 1959. It merged with the neighboring town of Jonesboro in 1947. The Temple Theatre, the Railroad House and the Lee County Courthouse are on the National Register of Historic Places. Brick buildings and older houses near downtown often have original floors and plaster, which dry differently from newer drywall.

If you own an older brick building downtown, tell us the age and use when you call. For businesses, our page on commercial water damage restoration explains phased drying, documentation and downtime.

Water setting

Little Buffalo Creek flows north through the center of the city, Big Buffalo Creek runs along the west side, and all of Sanford lies in the Cape Fear River watershed.

Emergency dispatch

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock for Sanford, including nights, weekends and holidays.

When water is spreading in Sanford

If water is moving, shut off the source and make the area safe first. Close the main valve, keep away from outlets in standing water, and stay out from under sagging ceilings. Then call. The line is open 24/7.

Stop the source

Close the main valve or the fixture valve.

Power safety

Cut power to the wet area at the breaker only if the panel itself is dry and easy to reach.

Lift valuables

Move electronics, files and stock off wet floors. Photograph the damage.

Call us

Tell us what is wet. We explain the plan, scope and price before work starts.

Creeks, runoff and local rules

Two creeks run through and beside Sanford, so low spots and areas near the creeks can take water in heavy rain. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your address. If water rose from outside, see flood damage cleanup.

Sanford is in Lee County, not Moore County, so permits come from a different office. The Building Permitting and Inspections Division is a City of Sanford and Lee County office that handles residential and commercial alterations and renovations. Ask it whether your rebuild scope needs a permit. For a wet floor or wall, see carpet water damage restoration and wall and drywall water damage repair.
