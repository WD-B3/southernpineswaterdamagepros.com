How to Clean Up Water Damage in Your Southern Pines Home

By the Southern Pines Water Damage Pros team

A small leak or an overflowing tub does not always need a crew. If the water was clean, the area is small and you act quickly, you can handle the first steps yourself. This guide explains those steps, the limits of doing it yourself, and the signs that the job has outgrown a household cleanup.

Start by deciding what kind of water it is

Water from a clean supply line, a rain leak through a ceiling or an overflowing sink is usually clean at first. Water from a toilet, a sewer drain or a flood is not. Contaminated water should not be cleaned up with household tools, because it carries bacteria and soaks into porous materials. If you are unsure, treat it as contaminated and call.

The first hour

  1. Stop the source. Close the shutoff valve or the main valve.
  2. Make it safe. Turn off power to the wet area at the breaker only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.
  3. Remove standing water with towels, a mop or a wet vacuum made for water.
  4. Lift rugs, toys and small furniture. Put foil or plastic under furniture legs on wet carpet.
  5. Photograph everything before you move it.

Do not use a regular household vacuum on wet floors. It can shock you and it can be ruined by water. A wet vacuum rated for liquids is the right tool.

The first 24 to 48 hours

EPA guidance on water damage says to dry wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to limit mold growth. That clock is the reason to move quickly. Open windows if humidity outdoors is lower than indoors, run fans across wet surfaces, and use a dehumidifier in the closed room.

  • Pull up wet area rugs and hang them to dry outdoors.
  • Remove wet baseboard trim if it is soft or swollen.
  • Move wet books, papers and fabrics to a dry room.
  • Wipe hard surfaces with a mild household cleaner after they dry.
  • Check behind furniture and inside closets for hidden wetness.

Water inside a wall is the case people most often miss. Moisture meters show wet drywall and framing that looks dry. For how professionals map and dry that moisture, read about structural drying, and for standing water across a room see water extraction.

Signs the job is bigger than you thought

  • The water has been there more than a day or two.
  • The carpet squishes when you step on it.
  • Baseboards or drywall feel soft or look swollen.
  • There is a musty smell that returns after you dry the surface.
  • Water came from a toilet, drain or outside.

Any of these suggests moisture in places a towel cannot reach. A quick call is cheaper than discovering a wet wall a month later. Call and describe what you see, and we explain next steps and the scope and price before any work begins.

Mistakes that make a wet house worse

  • Running a household vacuum over standing water, which is unsafe and ruins the machine.
  • Turning the heat up high and closing the house, which can raise humidity in the walls.
  • Leaving wet rugs and cushions piled together, where they stay wet for days.
  • Pulling out wet drywall before the source of the water is stopped.
  • Stepping into rooms with standing water while the power is still on.

Most of these come from good intentions. The goal in the first hours is to stop the source, make the area safe, protect belongings and let a drying plan start with clear readings. Anything beyond that can usually wait for a short phone call.

If you are unsure about any step, stop and call. A short conversation can save an afternoon of work that has to be redone.

Southern Pines utility note

The Town of Southern Pines Utilities Division says it serves more than 11,500 service connections with water distribution and sewer collection, and that sewer obstructions between the property line and the sewer main are the customer's responsibility unless caused by roots in the right-of-way or pipe failure. If your cleanup involves a backed-up drain, call the Town to find out which side of the property line the blockage is on.

Source type: Town of Southern Pines Utilities Division page. If you are in Carthage or Sanford, your utility and permit office differ, so see our pages for Carthage and Sanford.

Frequently asked questions

Can I clean up water damage myself?

For a small clean-water spill on a hard floor, usually yes. For soaked carpet, wet walls or contaminated water, a crew with meters and extraction equipment is the safer choice.

How quickly should I start drying?

As soon as the source is stopped. EPA guidance calls for drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to limit mold.

Is a regular shop vacuum okay for water?

Only a wet or dry model rated for liquids, and only on GFCI-protected outlets or battery power. A household vacuum is not safe.

What if the water came from my toilet?

Treat it as contaminated. Keep people out, do not clean it with household tools, and call a crew.

Who do I call in Southern Pines about a blocked sewer line?

The Town's Utilities Division says to call the Town so it can determine whether the blockage is on the customer side or the Town side of the property line.
